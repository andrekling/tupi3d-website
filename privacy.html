Tupi3D Privacy Policy

Last updated: 7 November 2026.

Tupi3D is a 3D modelling app for VR headsets. This policy explains what data the app handles, where it goes and how to have it removed. It covers the Tupi3D app and the website www.tupi3d.com.

The short version

  • You do not need an account to use Tupi3D, and we do not create one.
  • The app contains no advertising, no analytics and no tracking.
  • Your models and settings stay on your headset.
  • Your voice is turned into text on the headset. It is not recorded or sent anywhere for that.
  • The room cameras are read only when you press Capture room light in AR, and only while you turn. The camera images are not kept; a small, blurred light map of the room is saved on your headset.
  • Tupi3D keeps camera photos only if you turn on Allow room photos (off unless you turn it on) and then press Record room photos. Those photos stay on your headset and you can delete them at any time.
  • The headset's room scan is read on the headset to show you a faint outline of your real room and, in AR if you turn on Room occlusion, to hide virtual objects behind real things; with Live depth also on, so does the live depth map. Tupi3D does not keep either -- unless you press Make object from scan, which copies the room's shape (no images) into your scene like any model you make.
  • Data leaves your headset only when you use a feature that needs it: working with other people, live streaming, signing in to a service, browsing the web or downloading something.
  • We do not store any of your data on our servers.
  • If you apply for the beta on our website, what you type in the form is emailed to us, and kept only for inviting you.

What stays on your headset

  • Your projects, models, textures, renders, screenshots and camera videos.
  • Your settings, the display name you chose and your avatar's colour.
  • Voice notes you record: your voice, your hand and head movement and pictures of the menus you used. They are files in your project.
  • Sign-in tokens for Twitch, YouTube, Facebook and Dropbox, if you sign in. "Sign out" removes them.
  • The in-app browser's history, bookmarks and cookies.
  • A random number created when the app is installed. A session host can use it to keep a device out of their session. It says nothing about you or your headset.
  • The room-light estimate (a few colour and angle values), and a small, deliberately blurred 512x256 light map of the room, if you capture one.
  • Room photos and where the headset was for each, if you allowed and recorded them.

Removing the app, or clearing its data in the headset's settings, deletes all of it.

Your hands

Hand tracking is off unless you turn it on (App, Controls). With it on, the positions of your hands and fingers are used on the headset to work the app, in place of the controllers. They are not recorded. In a shared session the other people see where your hands are, as they do with controllers.

Your room's light

In AR, Capture room light (Quest 3/3S) reads the headset's forward cameras for one slow turn, about 10-20 seconds, to estimate how bright the room is, its colour, and where the main light comes from. The camera images are processed in memory and are never saved, recorded or sent anywhere. A small, deliberately blurred 512x256 light map is saved on your headset, and your project refers to it like any other HDRI; it is never uploaded. The cameras are read only while you turn, only after you press the button, and only while AR passthrough is on.

Your room's shape

Tupi3D asks the headset for the room understanding you made with Space Setup (the room scan). It uses it on the headset to draw a faint grey outline of your real walls and furniture, so you can see where you are even when not in AR (View > Room scan turns it off), and to give the room an invisible collision surface. In AR, if you turn on Room occlusion, the same scan hides virtual objects behind real furniture and walls; if you also turn on Live depth, it reads the live depth map to do the same for things the scan does not know about, such as people. Tupi3D does not record, store or send either of them; the room scan itself is made and kept by the headset, not by Tupi3D. The one exception is yours to make: Make object from scan copies the room's shape (triangles only, no camera images or colours) into your scene as an ordinary object. From then on it is part of your project like anything you model -- saved with it, and seen by the people in your session if you are in one -- until you delete it. Refine scan (Quest 3/3S, in AR) reads the live depth map a few times a second, only while it runs, to build a finer copy of the room on the headset. It is kept in memory only, and is gone when you clear it or close the app, unless you press Make object from refined scan, which works like Make object from scan. Rescan room opens the headset's own Space Setup. If you deny the system's spatial-data permission, these features stay off and the rest of the app works normally.

Room photos (only if you allow them)

Allow room photos, on the environment page, is off unless you turn it on. With it off, Tupi3D never keeps a camera image. With it on, and only while you are in AR and have pressed Record room photos, Tupi3D saves photos from one of the headset's front cameras. Each time you hold still somewhere new, it saves one photo, together with where the headset was and the camera's lens details (and, with the recording, the room scan's shape as a starting point for a splat). They are used to put the room's real look on your room scan and to make 3D captures ("splats") of it. A short click sounds for each photo, and the environment page shows how many are stored.

The photos stay in Tupi3D's private storage on your headset. Tupi3D does not send them anywhere, put them in your projects or show them to the people in a shared session. Turning the switch off or leaving AR stops a recording at once, and Delete room photos removes every one. Uninstalling the app also removes them.

The microphone

The microphone is off until you turn it on. With it on:

  • Voice commands and dictation are recognised on the headset. The audio is not kept and not sent.
  • In a multiplayer session, the other people in the session hear you.
  • In a live stream, your audience hears you, unless you mute it.
  • A voice note records you until you stop it.

Working with other people (multiplayer)

When you host or join a session, the other people in it receive:

  • your display name and avatar colour;
  • the position of your head and hands;
  • your voice, if your microphone is on;
  • the scene you are working on together, with its notes and drawings;
  • pictures of your menus and panels, only if you turn on "Share my panels and menus".

Sessions are private: someone needs your room code, your address or to be on your network to join. The host can mute, remove or ban a participant.

If you connect directly, the other devices can see your IP address. If you connect through our relay server, the relay sees it instead. The relay runs at Oracle Cloud in Frankfurt, Germany. It passes the session's data along and does not store or read it.

Session data is encrypted in transit. When you connect through our relay, the app checks that it is talking to the real relay. When you connect directly to a host, the link is encrypted but the app cannot verify who the host is, so join only hosts you know. Versions of the app from before 13 October 2026 did not encrypt sessions.

What you share with other people stays with them. We cannot remove it from their devices.

Live streaming

When you go live, the picture you see in the headset, the app's sound and your microphone (unless muted) are sent to the service you chose: Twitch, YouTube, Facebook or a server you entered. The headset asks for your permission to capture the screen each time.

The camera tool uses the same screen-capture permission to make a video: the headset view is encoded to an H.264 MP4 and either saved in your project or sent to the LocalSend receiver you chose. It is not sent anywhere else.

  • Twitch: you sign in on Twitch's own page. Tupi3D reads your stream key and chat, sets your stream's title, category and tags, and sends the chat messages you type. Twitch's privacy notice applies: https://www.twitch.tv/p/legal/privacy-notice/
  • Facebook: you can sign in on Facebook's own device page, or paste a server URL and stream key from Facebook Live Producer. Signing in lets Tupi3D read your name and create and end a live video on your account; it does not read your chat or viewer count. Meta's privacy notice applies: https://www.facebook.com/privacy/policy/
  • YouTube: Tupi3D uses YouTube API Services. You sign in on Google's own page. Tupi3D reads your channel's name, creates a stream key and a broadcast with your title and visibility, reads the viewer count and chat, and sends the chat messages you type. By using this feature you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service (https://www.youtube.com/t/terms). Google's privacy policy applies: https://policies.google.com/privacy. You can withdraw Tupi3D's access at any time at https://myaccount.google.com/permissions.

The sign-in details stay on your headset. We never receive them.

Other services you may use from the app

  • Dropbox, to open and save files there: https://www.dropbox.com/privacy
  • The in-app browser, including Discord and Poly Haven: each website receives what any browser sends it and applies its own policy.
  • Speech models you choose to download come from Hugging Face.
  • LocalSend sends the files you choose to a device you choose on your own network. When the camera tool has a connected receiver, the photo or video you take is sent to that device; with no receiver it is saved in your project instead.
  • PC Desk shows your own computer's screen in the app and sends it your clicks and typing. It talks only to Saci (our program for your computer, formerly called Tupi Desk) running on that computer, on your own network, encrypted, after you pair it with a password; the picture is shown and never saved, and nothing goes to us. In AR you can pair by looking at the code Saci shows: while the PC board is waiting, the headset's camera looks for that code; frames are read on the headset and dropped at once, never stored or sent. Once paired, the photos and videos you take in the app also go to that computer while Saci is running there, and a project goes to it (or comes back) when you press Send or Get. When you render with Cycles and "Render on my PC" is on, the scene you render goes to that computer to be rendered there, and the picture comes back; the computer deletes the scene when the render ends and keeps a copy of the picture in its Saci folder. Heavy modelling work (a voxel boolean, texture bakes, a remesh, a sculpt turned into clay or back into a mesh, sculpted clay following a layer, clay shapes being joined or blended) can go to it the same way: the shapes are sent and the result comes back; the computer keeps only the last few shapes, so they need not be sent again. When you cast your view to it ("Cast my view", or "Show headset view" in Saci, and you allow screen capture), what the headset shows -- with the app's sound, and your microphone if it is on for streaming -- goes to that computer, which shows it in a window and offers it to programs on that same computer (such as OBS); it is not saved unless you record it there.

Applying for the beta (our website)

The form at www.tupi3d.com asks for your name, the email address of your Meta account, your headset, your experience with 3D and, if you like, what you would make. When you send it, it goes to us as an email through Web3Forms, a form-to-email service (the website has no server of its own). Before it is sent, hCaptcha checks that you are a person, not a program; to do that it sees your IP address and how your browser behaves. Nothing is sent until you press the button.

We use what you send only to invite you to the beta and to write to you about it. We keep it until the beta ends, or until you ask us to delete it, whichever comes first.

What the app does on its own

When it starts, Tupi3D fetches a small file from www.tupi3d.com that lists our relay servers. No information about you is sent, but as with any web request the host (GitHub Pages) sees your IP address.

Why we may process this data

Under the EU General Data Protection Regulation: to provide the features you use (Article 6(1)(b)), with your consent for the microphone, screen capture, room-camera capture, room photos, spatial data, sign-ins and a beta application (Article 6(1)(a)), and our legitimate interest in running the relay and keeping sessions safe (Article 6(1)(f)).

Who else is involved

  • Oracle (cloud hosting of the relay, Frankfurt, Germany).
  • GitHub (hosting of the website, and of Saci's downloads).
  • Web3Forms (delivering a beta application to us by email).
  • hCaptcha, by Intuitive Machines, Inc., USA (the check that a beta application comes from a person).
  • The services you choose to use, listed above, each under its own policy.

We do not sell data and do not share it with anyone else.

How long data is kept

We keep nothing from the app. Data on your headset stays until you delete it or remove the app. A beta application is kept in our email until the beta ends or you ask us to delete it.

Deleting your data, and your rights

  • On your headset: sign out of each service in Stream settings and the Dropbox panel; delete projects in the Files panel; delete room photos on the environment page; or remove the app to delete everything.
  • With Twitch, Google or Dropbox: through your account with them.
  • With us: we hold no data about you, unless you applied for the beta (write to us and we delete it). If you believe we do, or to report a problem, write to andrekling@gmail.com and we will answer within 30 days.

You have the right to ask for access to, correction or deletion of your personal data, to restrict or object to its processing, and to complain to a data protection authority, for example the one where you live.

Children

Tupi3D is not intended for children under 13.

Three features are locked when the app is first installed, and are unlocked in the app's settings (App, Privacy & safety):

  • The web browser and live streaming can be unlocked only by someone who is 18 or older, or who has a parent's or guardian's permission.
  • Shared sessions (multiplayer), where other people can hear you and see what you make, can be unlocked only by someone who is 16 or older, or who has a parent's or guardian's permission.

Reporting other people

Everyone in a session is expected to follow our Code of Conduct. To report someone's behaviour, press the menu button on your controller and choose Report, use the Report button next to their name in the People list, or write to andrekling@gmail.com. The session's host can mute, remove or ban a participant, and you can mute or hide anyone for yourself.

Changes

We will update this page when the app changes what it does with data, and change the date at the top.
